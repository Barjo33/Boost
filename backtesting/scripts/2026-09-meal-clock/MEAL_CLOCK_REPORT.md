# When carbohydrate is announced, and how much


564,201 announcements from 1,028 participants across 2 corpora, placed on a single 24-hour axis.


## What an announcement is


At least 8 g, not entered at or below 4.4 mmol/L, entries close in time merged. That leaves a median of 2.0 announcements per participant-day.


| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| median announcement | 30 g |
| interquartile range | 17 to 45 g |
| 90th centile | 60 g |
| 99th centile | 103 g |
| announcements per participant-day | 2.0 |
| announced carbohydrate per participant-day | 65 g |

A median of 65 g a day is well below likely intake, and 89 per cent of participants announce under 100 g. Much of what these participants ate was never announced, so this is the announced fraction of eating rather than eating.

## How carbohydrate is entered


72 per cent of announcements are a multiple of 5 g and 43 per cent a multiple of 10. The six commonest values are 20 g (9.1%), 15 g (9.0%), 30 g (8.9%), 10 g (7.1%), 25 g (6.4%), 40 g (5.8%).


This is worth stating before any model is asked to predict a quantity. The announcement is a person's estimate rounded to a convenient number, so its own resolution is about 5 g and its accuracy is unknown. Whatever a trace could in principle reveal, it is scored against a target of that precision.


## How regular an individual is


Measured on the 996 participants with at least 60 announcements.


| measure | 10th centile | median | 90th centile |
|---|---|---|---|
| share of own announcements in own busiest 3 hours | 0.24 | 0.29 | 0.38 |
| circular concentration R | 0.29 | 0.39 | 0.50 |

The median participant announces 29 per cent of their carbohydrate within their own three busiest hours, and the range runs 0.18 to 0.67. Individuals are far less regular than the pooled pattern looks, which matters for anything built on a personal timing prior: the structure in the figure belongs mostly to the population.
