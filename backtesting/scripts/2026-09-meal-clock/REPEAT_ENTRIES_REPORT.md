# Repeated carbohydrate entries inside an hour, in the meal windows


809,314 entries of 8 g or more inside breakfast, lunch and dinner, from 1,030 participants. An entry counts as repeated if the same participant enters the same amount again inside 60 minutes.


| | rate |
|---|---|
| entries with a same-size partner in the window | 6.4% |
| the same, with sizes shuffled within participant | 3.7% |
| excess over chance | +2.7 percentage points |

The control matters more than the headline. Six amounts account for nearly half of all entries, so two entries an hour apart agree by luck often; without shuffling, the raw rate looks like a strong pattern and is mostly coincidence.


## How it varies between people


Across 1,012 participants with at least 50 entries in the windows.


| measure | 10th | median | 90th |
|---|---|---|---|
| observed rate | 0.5% | 4.1% | 11.8% |
| excess over own chance | -0.3 pp | +1.5 pp | +6.0 pp |

77 per cent of participants sit above their own chance rate and 14 per cent exceed it by more than five percentage points. So the behaviour is real and widespread but slight for most people, with a minority doing it a great deal.


## What the repeats look like


| gap between the pair | share |
|---|---|
| under 5 minutes | 27% |
| 5 to 15 | 21% |
| 15 to 30 | 22% |
| 30 to 60 | 29% |

Median gap 16 minutes. The amounts that repeat are small: 15 g, 10 g, 20 g, 30 g, 25 g, 8 g, against an overall median entry of 28 g.


That combination, small amounts repeated at short intervals, reads as one meal entered in parts rather than as a person eating the same thing twice. It is a reason to treat the small end of the size distribution as partly an artefact of entry behaviour, and a reason the merging step in the readability extraction matters more than it looks.
